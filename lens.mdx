The Lens shows what a range of snapshots does to your code before you read the diff. Each numbered step names the functions, classes and types it adds, changes or removes, and a canvas beside the steps draws those pieces and how they call each other.

## Open the Lens

Select a project and click **Lens**, next to **Changes** and **Map** at the top right. It's there once the project has a snapshot. The sidebar folds to icons while you read; **Changes** brings it back.

The Lens needs ShadowGit 0.3.6 or later.

## Pick a range

The **Range** menu, at the top left, picks which snapshots the Lens reads:

| Range | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Since last checkpoint** | From the newest snapshot back to your last checkpoint. The Lens opens on this one. |
| **This AI session** | Your assistant's latest session. It appears once the project has had one. |
| **Today** | Everything since midnight, in your local time. |

For a range of your own, shift-click two snapshots in the list below the menu. A plain click on a snapshot opens it in **Changes**.

New snapshots don't redraw what you're reading. When they arrive, **Newer snapshots: update** appears at the top. Click it to move the range up to the newest snapshot.

## Read it

- **The top** names the range and counts its snapshots, files, added and removed lines and changes. Below the counts are a summary and the checkpoints in the range, with their messages.
- **Each step** says what it adds, changes or removes, in order. Its names link to their cards on the canvas. **Also in this step** lists the other pieces it touches, and a line like "Reaches 14 symbols in 5 files" says how much code depends on it. **Show code** opens the step's code.
- **The canvas** draws a card for each function, class or type, marked **New**, **Changed** or **Removed**, with arrows for the calls between them. Layers run as captions across the top.

Scroll the steps and the canvas follows. Click a card to open its step, or press `J` and `K` to move to the next and previous step. **Where it lands**, in the canvas's corner, opens the [Map](/map) with this range lit on your whole project.

## Words from your assistant

ShadowGit writes the steps from the code on its own. The assistant that made the change can write them instead: a summary, a title and a sentence or two for each step, and the role each piece plays. At the end of a session, ask it:

```text
Write the Lens words for this session
```

The Lens then shows "Words by Claude Code · every name checked against the code graph" (or your assistant's name), and picks up the words within a few seconds, without a reload. Under **The author says**, a step can quote the checkpoint message behind it. A step your assistant didn't word says "From the code alone". This needs the [ShadowGit MCP server](/installation) set up in your assistant.

The words belong to that range. A piece's role also shows in later ranges while its code is unchanged. ShadowGit keeps the words in the project's `.shadowgit.git`, on your computer.

## What the Lens reads

The Lens reads code in TypeScript, JavaScript, Python, Java, C, C++, C#, Go, Rust, Kotlin, PHP, Ruby and Swift. Other files, such as docs, config and images, show in **Changes** but not in the Lens. A range that changes only those says "Nothing in this range changes code", with **Show the changes** to open them.

Limits:

- A snapshot of more than 20,000 files or 64 MB of source code is too large to draw, and so is a range whose two ends together pass that.
- Source files over 512,000 characters are skipped.
- A step shows the code of up to 12 files. Open the rest in **Changes**.

## Troubleshooting

When the Lens can't draw a range, it says "Couldn't draw this range" and why.

### "This snapshot has too many files for the code graph" or "This snapshot is too large for the code graph"

The project's snapshots hold more than 20,000 files or 64 MB of source. Generated code and copied-in dependencies are the usual cause. Add their folders to `.shadowgit-ignore` (see [Leave files out](/snapshots#leave-files-out)), and the next snapshots leave them out.

### "Drawing the Lens took longer than 120 s"

Pick a smaller range, with fewer snapshots or fewer changed files.
